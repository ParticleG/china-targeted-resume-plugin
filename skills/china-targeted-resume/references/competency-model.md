# Competency Model

A competency model translates role requirements into observable expectations. It is not a keyword list and never judges the candidate.

## Competency record

For each stable competency ID, retain:

- linked requirement IDs;
- capability dimension;
- expected depth (awareness, assisted execution, independent delivery, design/leadership, or source-specific wording);
- system scale and operating environment;
- responsibility scope and collaboration boundary;
- business/domain problem;
- expected validation signals;
- origin and source references.

Use source language where available. If depth, scale, team role, or responsibility is inferred, state the basis and uncertainty. “Target source does not state an exact scale” is preferable to invention.

## Hard gate versus bonus

Hard-gate status belongs to an explicitly blocking requirement or constraint, not to keyword frequency. Preferred capabilities and bonus signals remain optional. A role-family competency baseline is reusable across companies, but company-specific Required items and hard gates must come from that company's explicit sources.

## Validation signals

Describe what would demonstrate the capability in the role's context: production or reproducible project, design trade-off, debugging case, deployment, performance/reliability result, incident analysis, test evidence, documentation, or stakeholder delivery. Do not claim the candidate has a signal and do not turn a proposed practice project into current evidence.

## Separation from evidence and gaps

`competency-model.md` answers “what capable performance looks like.” `evidence-mapping.md` answers “what evidence currently exists.” `gap-analysis.md` answers “what impact an evidence deficit has.” Keep these questions separate so target expectations cannot leak into personal facts and candidate evidence cannot lower the target model.
